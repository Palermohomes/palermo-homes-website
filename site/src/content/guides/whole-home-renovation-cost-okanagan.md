What a whole-home renovation actually costs in Kelowna, West Kelowna, Lake Country, Peachland, Vernon, and Penticton in 2026. Pricing by scope level, by room, by renovation type, plus the hidden costs that catch most homeowners off guard.

Published October 2026 · By [Jas Sidhu](/about/jas-sidhu/), Owner of Palermo Homes · Custom Home Builder and Renovator in West Kelowna, BC

Whole-home renovations in Kelowna currently run $150 to $400 per square foot in 2026, depending on scope. For a 2,000 square foot home, that works out to roughly $300,000 for a cosmetic refresh at the lower end and $800,000 for a full gut-and-rebuild with high-end finishes. Costs in the Central Okanagan run about 5% above the Canadian national average. Scope and finish level are far bigger cost drivers than square footage alone.

Renovation is the question we get asked about more than any other, usually by homeowners who love their lot and location but want a home that lives like the ones being built new. The honest answer involves scope ranges, not a single number, because the same square footage can be renovated for a quarter of the top-end number depending on what the project actually touches.

This guide covers what a Kelowna whole-home renovation runs by scope level, by room type, how renovation compares to new construction, permit requirements, timeline, and the hidden costs that reliably appear once walls open up.

## Cost per square foot by scope level

Most Kelowna renovations fit into one of three scope buckets. Picking which bucket you are in before you start pricing is the single most important decision in setting a realistic budget.

| Scope level | Per sq ft | 2,000 sq ft total | What it includes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cosmetic refresh | $100 to $150 | $200,000 to $300,000 | Paint, flooring, light fixtures, cabinet fronts, minor finish updates |
| Mid-range renovation | $150 to $250 | $300,000 to $500,000 | New cabinets, countertops, appliances, plumbing fixtures, some layout changes |
| Full gut renovation | $250 to $400+ | $500,000 to $800,000+ | Strip to studs, new mechanical/electrical/plumbing, structural changes, new layout, high-end finishes |

The 5% Okanagan premium over the Canadian national average applies across all three scope levels. Trades, materials, and transportation into the valley all cost slightly more than in Lower Mainland or Calgary metros.

## Cost by room

Whole-home renovations rarely touch every room at the same intensity. Breaking down expected cost per room helps allocate budget to the spaces that matter most.

### Kitchen

Kitchens are consistently the most expensive room in a Kelowna renovation. Typical ranges:

| Kitchen tier | Cost range | Typical scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refresh | $18,000 to $30,000 | Cabinet refacing, new counters, backsplash, hardware, minor appliance updates |
| Mid-range | $30,000 to $60,000 | Semi-custom cabinets, quartz counters, full appliance package, same layout |
| Custom | $60,000 to $115,000+ | Full custom cabinets, natural stone, premium appliances, layout changes, custom millwork |

Average Kelowna kitchen project in 2026 runs approximately $50,000. The biggest cost multiplier is moving plumbing or gas (relocating a sink, dishwasher, or range), which triggers subfloor work, drywall patching, and inspection sign-offs on each affected trade.

### Bathrooms

Primary ensuite and shared bathrooms are typically the next-largest line items after the kitchen.

| Bathroom tier | Cost range | Typical scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refresh | $8,500 to $15,000 | Vanity swap, new fixtures, tile paint, new mirror and lighting |
| Mid-range | $15,000 to $30,000 | New vanity, tile floor and shower, new tub or shower, some fixture relocations |
| Custom | $30,000 to $55,000+ | Custom vanity, natural stone, freestanding tub, large glass enclosure, full layout change |

Average Kelowna bathroom in 2026 runs approximately $23,000 to $25,000. Moving the toilet is the single most expensive change in a bathroom renovation because it requires relocating the drain through the subfloor.

### Basement finishing

Basements come in three distinct tiers depending on whether the finished space is a legal secondary suite.

| Basement scope | Cost range | Typical includes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic finish | $25,000 to $40,000 | Framing, drywall, basic electrical, simple flooring, no bathroom |
| Full finish | $55,000 to $80,000 | 3-piece bathroom, bedroom with egress window, main living area, finished flooring |
| Legal secondary suite | $75,000 to $100,000+ | Separate entrance, kitchen, bathroom, dedicated HVAC, fire separation, egress compliance, permits |

Average Kelowna full basement finish in 2026 runs approximately $64,000. Legal secondary suites require specific permit review and inspection sign-offs that the City handles differently from standard renovation work.

### Other rooms

- **Bedrooms:** $40 to $100 per sq ft depending on scope (refresh vs full new flooring, trim, closet rebuild)
- **Living and family rooms:** $50 to $150 per sq ft (fireplace rework, built-ins, feature walls add quickly)
- **Mudroom and laundry:** $15,000 to $50,000 depending on cabinetry and fixture scope
- **Home office:** $10,000 to $35,000 with built-in cabinetry and lighting

## When renovation makes sense vs new construction

The question every Okanagan homeowner should ask before committing to a whole-home renovation: at what point does the renovation cost approach new-construction cost? Here is the rough comparison:

| Approach | Typical cost range | What you keep | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cosmetic refresh | $100 to $150 per sq ft | Everything structural + systems + layout | Visual update only |
| Mid-range renovation | $150 to $250 per sq ft | Structure, most systems, most layout | Updated finishes + partial system update |
| Full gut renovation | $250 to $400+ per sq ft | Structure, lot, possibly facade | Essentially new interior with current layout flexibility |
| New custom build | $600 to $1,500+ per sq ft | Lot only | Fully new, current code, Step Code 3+ efficiency, layout freedom |

Renovation wins when the existing home has strong bones (sound foundation, good roof, appropriate layout for current needs) and when the location is irreplaceable. New construction wins when the existing home has structural issues, when the layout cannot be made to work without moving load-bearing walls, or when the finished product needs to meet modern energy code and current layout expectations across the board. On a per-square-foot basis, full gut renovations approach the lower end of new-build cost, which is often the moment to re-ask the question.

## What permits are required for a Kelowna renovation

The City of Kelowna requires building permits for:

- Structural changes (removing or altering walls, beams, posts)
- Additions of any kind
- Decks
- New or altered walls (even non-structural)
- Plumbing work (new fixtures or fixture relocations)
- Electrical work (handled by Technical Safety BC permits)
- HVAC changes (ducting modifications, equipment upgrades)
- Changes to window or door openings
- Secondary suite creation or legalization

Purely cosmetic work (paint, carpet, cabinet refacing without plumbing or electrical changes) generally does not require a permit. When in doubt, confirm with the municipality before any work begins. West Kelowna, Lake Country, Peachland, Penticton, and Vernon all have similar permit triggers with municipality-specific fee schedules.

## Timeline: how long a whole-home renovation takes

Renovation timelines vary more than most people expect, because scope varies more than most people expect.

| Scope level | Design + permits | Construction | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cosmetic refresh | 2 to 6 weeks | 6 to 12 weeks | 2 to 4 months |
| Mid-range renovation | 6 to 12 weeks | 12 to 24 weeks | 4 to 8 months |
| Full gut renovation | 8 to 16 weeks | 24 to 40 weeks | 8 to 14 months |

Occupied renovations (where the owner remains in the home during construction) typically add 2 to 4 months to the construction timeline because trades work around daily use, dust containment is more complex, and critical systems need to stay operational.

## The hidden costs that reliably show up

Every renovation reveals surprises once walls open. The five we see most often in Okanagan homes:

1. **Outdated wiring.** Knob-and-tube in pre-1950s homes or aluminum wiring in some 1960s-70s homes requires full rewire. Cost: $15,000 to $40,000+ for a typical 2,000 square foot home.
2. **Outdated plumbing.** Galvanized steel supply lines, cast iron drains, or poly-B lines often fail inspection once exposed. Full replumb: $10,000 to $30,000+ depending on access.
3. **Asbestos.** Common in insulation, drywall mud, floor tile adhesive, and some pipe wrap on homes built before approximately 1990. Testing is required before demolition in BC; abatement adds $5,000 to $25,000 depending on scope.
4. **Foundation moisture or structural issues.** Previously hidden by finished walls, these can require waterproofing, structural reinforcement, or in rare cases underpinning.
5. **Inadequate electrical service.** Older homes with 60-amp or 100-amp service often need to upgrade to 200-amp to support modern renovations. Panel upgrade: $3,000 to $8,000, plus any required service upgrade from BC Hydro.

A pre-renovation assessment that includes a walk-through by the builder, electrician, and plumber (ideally with target wall sections opened for inspection) catches most of these before contract and lets them be priced accurately rather than appearing as change orders mid-construction.

## What drives the final number up or down

Two homes of identical square footage can come in at very different totals. The main variables:

- **Finish level.** Builder-grade vs mid-range vs premium can swing per-square-foot cost by 50% or more
- **Cabinet selection.** Stock vs semi-custom vs full custom is a 2-3x cost multiplier on a typical kitchen
- **Appliance package.** Entry-level vs premium vs commercial-style can add $20,000 to $50,000+ in a single room
- **Layout changes.** Moving plumbing, electrical, and HVAC adds significantly beyond pure finish work
- **Structural changes.** Removing load-bearing walls requires engineering, beams, and inspection sign-offs
- **Age of home.** Older homes almost always surface more hidden costs during demolition
- **Access.** Steep driveways, narrow sites, and limited material staging all add labour hours

## How to budget realistically before signing anything

1. Pick a scope level based on what the home actually needs, not what feels affordable
2. Get a professional pre-renovation walk-through with any contractor you are considering
3. Request itemized pricing (not a single number) covering demo, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, finishes, and project management separately
4. Build in a 15% contingency on top of the quoted number for hidden-cost discoveries
5. Confirm what permits will be required and factor fees + Development Cost Charges where applicable
6. Clarify whether the home will be occupied during construction and factor that into timeline + daily rate

This guide reflects current renovation cost conditions and permit requirements in the Central Okanagan as of 2026. Material costs, labour rates, and municipal fee schedules change. Verify current specifics with the relevant municipality and any contractor you interview before finalizing a budget.
