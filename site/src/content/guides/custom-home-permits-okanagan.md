A plain-English walk-through of the building permit process for a custom home in Kelowna, West Kelowna, Lake Country, Peachland, Penticton, and Vernon. What it costs, what you need to submit, how long each municipality takes, and the hidden charges most homeowners do not budget for.

Published October 2026 · By [Jas Sidhu](/about/jas-sidhu/), Owner of Palermo Homes · Custom Home Builder in West Kelowna, BC

Building permit fees in Kelowna and West Kelowna are calculated at approximately 1.14% of construction value (minimum $100), which puts the base permit for a $1.5 million custom home at around $17,000. On top of that, expect $15,000 to $35,000+ in Development Cost Charges, $2,000 to $4,000 for Energy Step Code reporting, mandatory Home Warranty registration, and separate permits for plumbing, electrical, and gas. Review times range from 15 business days in West Kelowna to 4 to 6 weeks or longer in Kelowna depending on application completeness and whether a Development Permit is required.

## The four phases of the custom home permit process

Every custom home in BC runs through the same four permit phases. Each has a typical duration, a set of required documents, and a set of things that can delay it.

| Phase | Typical duration | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-application | 2 to 6 weeks | Drawings finalized, engineering stamped, Step Code report completed, Home Warranty registration, geotech if required |
| Development Permit (if required) | 4 to 10 weeks | Design-guideline review for hillside, hazardous conditions, or planned community lots |
| Building Permit review | 2 to 6 weeks | Municipal technical review, revisions, issuance |
| Trade permits + inspections | Ongoing through construction | Plumbing, electrical (Technical Safety BC), gas, and construction-phase inspections |

The compound total ranges from 6 weeks on a straightforward flat-lot build in West Kelowna with a complete submission, to 4+ months on a hillside lot in Kelowna with a Development Permit, Step Code revisions, and structural review comments.

## Building permit fees by municipality

Most Central Okanagan municipalities calculate building permit fees as a percentage of construction value. The percentages are similar across jurisdictions, but each municipality publishes its own fee bylaw with specific minimums and application fees.

### City of Kelowna

Kelowna's current fee structure is tiered by construction value, with a standard rate of approximately 1.14% of construction value and a minimum of $100. For higher-value custom homes the actual bylaw tiers are:

- $1 to $10,000: $175.00 minimum
- $10,001 to $100,000: $175.00 plus $9.55 per $1,000
- $100,001 to $500,000: $1,034.50 plus $8.05 per $1,000 over $100,000
- $500,001 and up: $4,254.50 plus $7.20 per $1,000 over $500,000

For a $1.5 million custom home that works out to approximately $11,500 using the tiered structure, or about $17,000 using the 1.14% calculation. Builders should confirm the current applicable rate directly with the City at application.

### District of West Kelowna

West Kelowna uses the same 1.14% of actual construction value calculation (minimum $100), plus a non-refundable application fee with a minimum of $150 due at the time of application. The permit fee itself is due at the time of permit issuance. For a $1.2 million custom home, the base permit fee runs approximately $13,700 on top of the $150+ application fee.

### District of Peachland, City of Penticton, City of Vernon, District of Lake Country

These municipalities each publish their own fee schedules. The percentage-of-construction-value model is common across BC but the specific percentage, minimum fee, and tier breakpoints vary. For current rates in these jurisdictions, consult the municipality's current Fees and Charges Bylaw before submission.

## Required documents checklist

A typical complete single-family application in BC requires:

- **Building Permit Application Form:** municipality-specific form
- **Current legal survey plan:** recent, stamped by a BC Land Surveyor
- **Site plan:** final design with building footprint, setbacks, grades, drainage, driveway, and services
- **Architectural drawings:** floor plans, elevations, sections, dimensions, door and window sizes and locations
- **Structural engineering:** stamped drawings from a Professional Engineer (P.Eng.) for foundations, framing, load paths
- **Mechanical, electrical, plumbing layout:** showing fixtures, mechanical equipment locations, panel locations
- **BC Energy Step Code Pre-Construction Report:** signed by a qualified Energy Advisor
- **Home Warranty Insurance confirmation:** New Home Registration from BC Housing, or Owner-Builder Exemption
- **Property Declaration Form:** ownership and intended use
- **Geotechnical Report:** required for hillside lots, slope-adjacent lots, or lots with known soil issues (see our [hillside guide](/guides/hillside-custom-home-kelowna/))
- **Development Permit documentation:** if the lot is in a DP Area (hillside, hazardous conditions, waterfront, architectural guideline community)

Waterfront or lakefront lots on Okanagan Lake, Kalamalka Lake, or Wood Lake typically require additional provincial reviews including Riparian Areas Protection Regulation (RAPR) compliance and in some cases archaeological assessment.

## BC Energy Step Code: what it is and what it costs

The BC Energy Step Code is a performance-based energy efficiency standard that applies to new construction across the province. Central Okanagan municipalities currently require most new homes to meet Step 3 at minimum. The code sets specific targets for airtightness, insulation levels, and mechanical system efficiency.

Compliance requires two reports from a qualified Energy Advisor:

- **Pre-Construction Report** ($800 to $2,500 depending on home size and complexity). Demonstrates the design will meet the required Step at completion. Required at building permit application.
- **Mid-construction airtightness test:** blower door test performed after framing and air barrier are complete
- **As-built verification report** ($600 to $1,500). Confirms the completed home meets Step requirements. Required at occupancy.

Clients who want to exceed Step 3 (for example to qualify for CleanBC rebates or future-proof against stricter future requirements) can target Step 4 or 5. Higher Steps add construction cost but can significantly reduce lifetime energy bills.

## Home Warranty Insurance (2-5-10)

BC's 2-5-10 New Home Warranty, administered through BC Housing, is a Provincial requirement for all new homes built by a licensed residential builder in BC. The warranty provides:

- **2 years** on labour and materials for defects
- **5 years** on building envelope (walls, roof, windows against water penetration)
- **10 years** on structural defects

The builder is responsible for registering the home with BC Housing and issuing the New Home Registration certificate that is required for building permit issuance. The warranty premium is typically in the range of $1,500 to $4,000 depending on home value and builder record.

**Palermo Homes is BC Housing Licensed Residential Builder Number 42812.** Every home we build is registered under our license and covered by 2-5-10 warranty automatically.

Owner-builders who intend to live in the home personally (not build-for-sale) can apply for an Owner-Builder Exemption through BC Housing. This waives the mandatory warranty but adds restrictions, including the ability to only build one owner-exempt home every 18 months and restrictions on selling the home for the first year after occupancy.

## Development Cost Charges (DCCs) and other hidden fees

Development Cost Charges are the fee most first-time custom home buyers do not budget for. DCCs are municipal fees collected at building permit issuance to fund off-site infrastructure improvements (roads, water, sewer, parks, drainage) attributable to new development. They are separate from and in addition to the building permit fee itself.

DCCs vary by municipality and by lot location within the municipality (sector-based pricing). In Kelowna, DCCs for a single-family home commonly run $15,000 to $35,000+ depending on the sector. West Kelowna, Peachland, Lake Country, Vernon, and Penticton each publish their own DCC bylaws with sector-specific rates.

Other charges that stack on top of base permit fees include:

- **Plumbing permit:** municipal, typically a flat or per-fixture fee
- **Electrical permit:** issued by Technical Safety BC, not the municipality
- **Gas permit:** if gas appliances are installed
- **Water and sewer connection fees:** if the lot is not already serviced
- **Driveway and curb cut permits:** if a new driveway accesses a municipal road
- **Tree protection or removal fees:** some municipalities require tree surveys and preservation deposits

Total permit-related costs on a Kelowna or West Kelowna custom home typically land between $18,000 and $40,000+ once all municipal and provincial charges are added together. That is a line item worth getting a full breakdown on from your builder before the budget is finalized.

## Realistic review timelines by municipality

Permit review times across the Central Okanagan vary significantly. These are the current published targets or best-available estimates by municipality:

- **District of West Kelowna:** 15 business days (about 3 weeks) for a complete single-family or duplex application. Published municipal target. Housing Incentive Program projects target 5 business days.
- **City of Penticton:** 21 business days (about 4 weeks) standard, or 1 to 14 business days for eligible applications through the Residential Xpress program. Published municipal target.
- **District of Peachland:** Approximately 2 weeks per the municipal building department FAQ, first-come first-served, dependent on application completeness.
- **City of Kelowna:** No published day-count target. Industry estimates run 4 to 6 weeks for complete applications; complex files take longer. The city operates a Fast Track program for eligible applications.
- **City of Vernon:** No published day-count target. Recent council reporting has indicated permit processing in the "green zone" with past averages under 4 weeks.
- **District of Lake Country:** No current day-count target published. Historical news coverage has noted periodic backlogs. Confirm current status directly with the municipality.

These targets assume complete applications. Revisions during review, incomplete submissions, or files that trigger referrals to other departments (planning, engineering, environmental, servicing) all extend the timeline. ## The five most common permit delays

Across hundreds of permit applications over 15+ years, these are the delays we see most often:

1. **Incomplete application submission.** Missing survey, missing Step Code report, missing structural stamp, missing warranty registration. Each triggers a hold and often a resubmission.
2. **Structural comments requiring revisions.** If the municipal reviewer flags structural concerns, the structural engineer must revise, re-stamp, and resubmit. Each round adds 2 to 6 weeks.
3. **Development Permit requirement discovered mid-review.** Hillside, Hazardous Conditions, or architectural guideline requirements that were not identified upfront can add 4 to 10 weeks.
4. **Referrals to other departments.** Planning, environmental, servicing, or engineering referrals trigger additional review cycles.
5. **Property-specific overlays.** Riparian areas (lakefront or stream-adjacent), archaeological overlays, agricultural land reserve adjacencies, or tree preservation requirements that were not factored into the application.

A well-prepared application assembled by a builder and design team who regularly build in the specific municipality usually clears first review with minor comments only. Permit speed is largely about preparation quality, not about advocacy or expediting.

## How to prepare for a clean first review

If you want your Kelowna or West Kelowna custom home permit to clear first review:

1. Confirm with the municipality whether a Development Permit applies to your lot before design finalizes
2. Commission the geotechnical report early if the lot has any slope or soil complexity
3. Engage a qualified Energy Advisor before design is complete so Step Code targets inform the design, not retrofit it
4. Have your structural engineer coordinate directly with the architectural team on load paths, not after drawings are complete
5. Submit the full package in one application, not in stages
6. Choose a builder who regularly builds in the specific municipality and knows that municipality's reviewer preferences

None of these prevent revisions entirely. They do prevent the preventable ones.
